# AI Agents Earning Real Money from Software Bounties and Security Bug Bounties (as of 7 Oct 2026)

How the research was done: WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for every primary source tried (dev.to, xbow.com, bleepingcomputer.com, breachloom.com, stingrai.io). **All findings below come from WebSearch result snippets and search-engine summaries, not full-page reads.** Exact figures should be spot-checked before anyone quotes them. "Self-reported" marks figures from the operator or vendor that made the claim, and "vendor" marks platform-published statistics.

---

## Q1. Is there documented evidence of an AI agent with light human supervision earning ≥$200/month from bounties? How reliable is it?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only weakly. Code bounties have one self-reported single-operator case: about $500–800 from 240 agent PRs over roughly 96 hours to a few weeks starting late May 2026. It is unaudited, and its two write-ups disagree with each other. Security has stronger evidence that AI systems produce real paid-tier results: XBOW on HackerOne (2025), Big Sleep, Claude Mythos, and AIxCC prize money. Those were all well-funded corporate or academic teams with human review of every report, not a lone autonomous agent. The individual "AI-assisted hunter" earnings found are self-reported, from humans driving Claude Code, not from autonomous agents.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Open-source and code bounties: agent-operator reports**
- A developer ran the "Hermes Agent" from late May 2026, telling it to find bounties and submit PRs. Result: 240 PRs, 72 merged, 90 closed, 78 still open, with estimated earnings of **$500–800 ("bounties + tokens")**. Self-reported, and I saw snippets only. — [DEV Community: "I Sent 240 PRs to Open Source Repos Using an AI Agent"](https://dev.to/zeroknowledge0x/i-sent-240-prs-to-open-source-repos-using-an-ai-agent-heres-the-brutal-truth-about-what-actually-16ic)
- A second write-up of what appears to be the same experiment carries the headline "96 Hours of Autonomous Bounty Hunting: My AI Agent Earned $800 on GitHub". Its body gives a $500–800 range and a start date of May 28 rather than May 24. Its daily table shows $0 for days 1–2. The headline and body are inconsistent, and "tokens" (crypto) count toward the total. — [AgentUpdate.ai](https://agentupdate.ai/news/ai-agent-github-bounty-96-hours-experiment)
- The same author's "Open Source Money Map 2026" piece exists, but its content was not retrievable. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/zeroknowledge0x/the-open-source-money-map-every-way-developers-are-actually-making-money-in-2026-with-real-45ba)
- An open-source "autonomous AI agency" project merged a bounty-hunter feature on about 4 Oct 2026. It searches Algora and Opire for funded issues and drafts fixes, and it opens an upstream PR with `/claim` only after the repo owner adds a `bounty:approved` label, so there is no auto-submit. The author expects low yield. This shows the tooling exists, not that anyone earned money. — [GitHub PR #1664 strikersam/autonomous-ai-agency](https://github.com/strikersam/autonomous-ai-agency/pull/1664)
- No official data from Algora, Opire, or IssueHunt on payouts to agent-submitted PRs was found. — (searches across the [Algora GitHub](https://github.com/algora-io/algora) and [gigs.sh Algora "agent earning guide"](https://gigs.sh/p/algora) returned none)

**(b) SWE-Lancer benchmark (OpenAI, Feb 2025): what "could" be earned on real Upwork/Expensify tasks**
- The dataset holds 1,488 real freelance tasks from the Expensify open-source repo, posted on Upwork and worth $1,000,000 in total payouts. — [arXiv 2502.12115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12115)
- The best model in the original paper, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, earned **$208,050 of $500,800** on the Diamond subset: 26.2% of individual-contributor (IC) coding tasks and 44.9% of management tasks. It earned **over $400,000 of $1,000,000** on the full set. — [arXiv 2502.12115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12115); [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/openais-new-benchmark-tests-ai-models-against-real-world-software-engineering-tasks/)
- A later blog reports these dollar-weighted IC Diamond results: GPT-5 about $112K, o3 about $86K, GPT-4.1 about $34K. I could not verify these against OpenAI's primary sources, and they are not comparable to the paper's headline numbers without knowing the setup. — (search summary, secondary blog, URL not surfaced; treat as unverified)
- Caveat: SWE-Lancer measures offline grading against hidden end-to-end tests on tasks that were already solved. It does not capture live competition, proposal acceptance, or being first to submit. — [arXiv 2502.12115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12115)
- A related paper ("Can AI Freelancers Compete? Benchmarking Earnings, Reliability, and Task Success at Scale", May 2025) exists. I did not read it. — [arXiv 2505.13511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13511)

  - Human review: XBOW says discovery was fully automated, but **its human security team reviewed 100% of reports before submission** to comply with HackerOne's automated-tool policy. — [XBOW "Top 1"](https://xbow.com/blog/top-1-how-xbow-did-it); [Rawsec](https://blog.raw.pm/en/about-the-hype-around-xbow/)

### Inferences
- The ≥$200/month threshold has been met only in **self-reported, single-operator, short-window** code-bounty anecdotes, about $500–800 total. The author's headline overstates the body, and "tokens" are included. Treat reliability as low. There is no evidence the result was sustained month over month.
- In security, AI clearly finds real, payable bugs, but every verified high-profile case involved **corporate-scale infrastructure plus 100% human review**. XBOW's resolution rate was about 13%, with no disclosed revenue. Nothing documents a lone autonomous agent clearing $200/month net on security bounties.
- SWE-Lancer's "$400K of $1M" reads as an upper bound under ideal offline conditions. It ignores competition, being first, proposal acceptance on Upwork, and maintainer trust, which are the binding constraints in practice.

### Gaps
- XBOW's actual dollar earnings, and whether most of its reports went to VDPs (no bounty) or paid programs, were not found.
- Primary data on agent-won bounties from Algora, Opire, IssueHunt, or Gitcoin does not exist publicly as far as I could find.
- Later SWE-Lancer results (GPT-5, Claude 4.x) were not verified against primary sources, and arXiv 2505.13511 was not read.
- No reports were found of Cal.com, ZIO/Ziverge, or Golem bounties being won by AI agents. Ziverge reportedly paid about $143K via Algora in total, from an aggregator figure. — [gigs.sh](https://gigs.sh/p/algora)

## Q2. What are the expected value, competition, and reputational or ban risks for a newcomer account?

### Takeaway
Competition is saturated and platform and maintainer hostility to unreviewed AI submissions has sharply increased through 2026. curl killed its bounty in January, HackerOne paused the IBB in March, Code4rena shut down in May, and Google paused the OSS VRP on 1 Oct 2026. Immunefi bans AI-generated reports outright, and tinygrad and others close or ban suspected-AI PRs. For a new, unreputed autonomous account, the realistic expected value is close to $0–$100 per month. The tail risk of account bans, and of reputational damage to the owner's GitHub and KYC identity, is material.

### Cited Findings

**Code-bounty competition and economics**
- An Algora $100 bounty gets about 11 competing PRs within 2 hours, according to the 240-PR experimenter, who called Algora "the worst offender". — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/zeroknowledge0x/i-sent-240-prs-to-open-source-repos-using-an-ai-agent-heres-the-brutal-truth-about-what-actually-16ic)
- A repo analysis (single unverified author) claims 98.9% of the about $1.07M advertised on Algora bounty issues sits in repos that merged 0 of 20,000+ PRs, meaning much advertised bounty money may be effectively unclaimable. — (search summary of a GitHub repo analysis; primary URL not surfaced; unverified)
- Algora fees were 23% in 2023 (20% Algora + 3% Stripe). That figure may be outdated. Algora has pivoted toward hiring ("Hire the top 1% OSS engineers"). — [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35829168); [algora-io/algora](https://github.com/algora-io/algora)
- Algora's own org shows 17 bounties totalling $3,300, with the top solver earning $1,375. No platform-wide total is published. — [Algora](https://algora.io/algora/home); [gigs.sh](https://gigs.sh/p/algora)
- Opire, per an aggregator: about 5,590 users, about 12K projects installed, Stripe USD payouts, 4% fee. — [gigs.sh / search summary](https://gigs.sh/p/algora)
- IssueHunt takes a 10% fee and has pivoted toward Japanese security bug bounties. OSS issue activity there is "relatively sparse". — [OSS.Fund](https://www.oss.fund/issue-hunt/); [IssueHunt readme](https://github.com/IssueHunt/readme)
- Polar v2.0 disabled issue funding for new signups and for existing customers without donations. Polar now focuses on billing. — [Polar discussion #3998](https://github.com/orgs/polarsource/discussions/3998)
- Expensify still posts $250 fixed-price Upwork jobs tied to GitHub issues. The proposal must be accepted before coding. Expensify itself uses AI internally (a "MelvinBot" PR account, AI-review checklist items). No Expensify ban on AI PRs was found. — [Upwork listing](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/250-Transaction-details-appear-top-batched-report-44226-Expensify_~0106429d3635864e62/); [Expensify/App PR #103115](https://github.com/Expensify/App/pull/103115)

One vendor blog says Upwork reply rates fell from about 4% (2023) to about 2% (2026) amid AI proposal floods. That is a vendor source and biased. — [GigUp HQ](https://giguphq.com/blog/upwork-ai-proposals-banned-2026); [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/ai-proposals-upwork)

Ghostty limits AI contributions to pre-approved issues. — [Generative Labs](https://www.generativelabs.com/insights/open-source-ai-contribution-rules); [InfoQ Feb 2026](https://infoq.com/news/2026/02/ai-floods-close-projects); [DevGenius](https://blog.devgenius.io/open-source-projects-are-now-banning-ai-generated-pull-requests-8e1dd3e8d41c?gi=d175b3440a3b)

- **Code4rena** announced its wind-down on 13 May 2026, with Immunefi absorbing its clients and wardens. Sources give the end date as 31 May or 12 Jul 2026, so they conflict. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-05-13-immufefi-absorb-code4rena-bug-bounty-customers-shutdown-decision-401179); [CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/05/13/code4rena-announces-wind-down-after-securing-billions-in-defi/)
- **Sherlock:** no explicit AI ban found in its public docs. It requires a PoC where an issue is unclear and counts only Medium/High findings. Watsons below the payout threshold lose judging-reward credit. — [Sherlock docs: validity criteria](https://docs.sherlock.xyz/audits/judging/guidelines); [Sherlock: scoring](https://docs.sherlock.xyz/audits/watsons/how-to-score-issue-points-in-a-contest)

### Inferences
- **Expected value for a newcomer autonomous agent (my estimate, not sourced):**
  - Code bounties: with about 11 PRs per $100 Algora bounty, a maintainer-trust penalty on new accounts, and AI-PR bans in high-value repos (tinygrad), a fair share is roughly 1 win in 10–20 attempts at $50–$250 each. Net of the Algora fee and LLM or compute cost, that is plausibly **$0–$150/month**. It is highly variable and front-loaded by luck.
  - Security bounties on mainstream web platforms: XBOW-class infrastructure achieved about 13% resolved. Hackbot findings skew to XSS, which is the most duplicated class. New accounts face HackerOne signal and reputation gating, and an Immunefi ban for AI reports. A lone agent submitting without careful human verification likely has **negative expected value** (lost signal, bans).
  - With an owner doing genuine human review, occasional $250–$2,000 wins are documented for skilled humans. They are not documented for an autonomous newcomer agent.
- **Reputational and ban risk is concentrated on the owner's identity.** KYC links bans to a real person. GitHub org bans (tinygrad), Immunefi bans, and HackerOne signal penalties carry over to any future human use of that account.
- The direction of travel is toward more restriction (curl, IBB, Google OSS VRP, Code4rena all paused or closed in 2026). The window for low-effort AI submissions is closing, while bounty programs increasingly reward verified, PoC-backed reports.

### Gaps
- I did not verify primary HackerOne, Bugcrowd, or Intigriti policy pages, or the exact HackerOne 22 Jul 2026 signal-requirement text.
- No quantitative data was found on the average number of attempts per bounty beyond the one anecdote of 11 PRs within 2 hours.
- Elastic's "$2 per report" policy was seen only as a headline; details are unknown.
- Gitcoin's current bounty status (it historically shifted from bounties to grants) was not researched in this pass. Bountysource shut down in 2023 (background knowledge; not re-sourced here).

## Q3. Which sub-niches remain under-competed?

### Takeaway
I found no source that rigorously identifies an under-competed niche. Indirect signals point to a few: bounties needing hardware or toolchain setup, or deep context (e.g., tinygrad performance and MLPerf bounties of $200–$600+, though tinygrad is hostile to AI PRs). Also Expensify-style Upwork work, where proposal acceptance gates entry, and supply-chain-scope security reports (still open at Google OSS VRP). Smart-contract audit contests are shrinking (Code4rena gone) and partly hostile (Immunefi ban, Code4rena V12 exclusion). Sherlock and Cantina do not explicitly ban AI-assisted human work.

### Cited Findings
- tinygrad bounties: "CUDA tensor core support … easy $200", and MLPerf training bounties raised to $600. The program was "back now that AI spam has died down", but low-effort AI work is unwelcome. — [tiny corp on X (2023)](https://x.com/__tinygrad__/status/1722480953854136364); [tiny corp on X (recent)](https://x.com/__tinygrad__/status/2090277911618293895)
- Expensify $250 Upwork issues require a proposal to be accepted before code is written. That filters out mass PR spam but needs an Upwork account with human-sent proposals. — [Upwork listing](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/250-Transaction-details-appear-top-batched-report-44226-Expensify_~0106429d3635864e62/); [GigUp HQ](https://giguphq.com/blog/upwork-ai-proposals-banned-2026)

### Inferences
- The most defensible approach for an ethical single agent is **not** mass bounty-sniping. Better options are:
  - narrow, high-context niches (less popular languages and ecosystems such as Scala/ZIO, Rust, Elixir, or bounties needing a specific build or toolchain setup that generic agents skip);
  - quality over volume, with an owner-reviewed PoC for every security report;
  - programs that explicitly welcome AI assistance with disclosure.
- Audit contests are a poor fit for a newcomer agent. The largest venue is gone, Immunefi bans AI-generated reports, AI auditors showed 0 valid findings in an independent test, and Sherlock's payout threshold punishes low-signal participants.
- Any niche that becomes known as profitable for agents will saturate quickly. Algora went from bounty to 11 PRs in 2 hours.

### Gaps
- No dataset on bounty competition by language or ecosystem was found, so the niche recommendations are inference.
- Cantina's and CodeHawks' AI policies were not found.
- No evidence was found for or against AI agents winning Cal.com, Golem, or ZIO bounties specifically.
