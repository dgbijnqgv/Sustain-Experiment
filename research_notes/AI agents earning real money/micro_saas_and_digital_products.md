# Micro-SaaS and Digital Products Built Largely by AI: Verified Revenue, Distribution, Effort and Timelines (as of 7 Oct 2026)

Method note: WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for every primary source I tried (trustmrr.com, indiehackers.com, news.ycombinator.com, dev.to, automatonagency.com, money-lab.app). **Everything below comes from WebSearch result summaries/snippets, not full-page reads**, so wording may be paraphrased. Each figure is dated where the snippet gave a date. Labels: **[VERIFIED]** means a payment processor (Stripe API) or platform backs it; **[SELF-REPORTED]** means founder/operator claim; **[ESTIMATE]** means third-party modeling; **[VENDOR]** means a source that sells something related (courses, tools, platforms). This is a heavily survivorship-biased area: success stories get posted and failures mostly don't. Many "how much can you earn" pages are written by people selling courses, templates or platforms.

## Q1. What is the realistic probability and timeline for a new micro-product to hit $200/month net? What is the distribution bottleneck, and which channels solve it without spam?

### Takeaway
No clean base rate exists. The best verified dataset (TrustMRR, Stripe-API-verified, about 7k+ startups) shows about two thirds of listed startups in the lowest "$0–$1k" band. That is already a selected group of founders confident enough to list. Non-verified surveys suggest very roughly 40–60% of micro-SaaS reach any revenue at all, with a typical 3–12 months to the first dollar. Every documented attempt in which an autonomous agent ran the full loop (2025–2026) ended between $0 and $54 total, even with heavy building and posting. Distribution, not building, is the consistent bottleneck. A realistic prior for an unknown, autonomous, no-spam micro-product reaching $200/month net within 3–6 months is low, plausibly well under 20%. That number is my inference, not a measured figure.

**Programmatic SEO as a channel (Google enforcement)**
- Google's spam policy defines scaled content abuse as generating many pages "primarily to manipulate rankings," including pages made with generative AI "without adding value" and pages scraped from feeds or search results, "regardless of how it was made." — [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); [Google Search Central blog, March 2024](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)
- Timeline. March 2024: the policy launched, and manual actions deindexed hundreds of sites. March 2026 core update: reportedly targeted mass unedited AI pages, template-variable substitution and aggregators that add no context, apparently algorithmically and without Search Console messages. June 2026 spam update: rolled out 24–26 June. August 2026 spam update: completed 21 Aug, with Glenn Gabe case studies including programmatic/scaled content. 28 Aug 2026: Google changed site-reputation-abuse enforcement in the EEA. Vendor-reported traffic losses for hit sites range from 40% to 90%, and none of those figures come from Google. — [Digital Applied: pSEO after March 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/programmatic-seo-after-march-2026-surviving-scaled-content-ban); [GSQI: August 2026 spam update](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/august-2026-google-spam-update-case-studies/); [Google: site reputation update Aug 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/08/update-site-reputation-policy)
- AI Overviews further cut clicks to informational/tool queries. Ahrefs found about a 58% CTR drop for position-1 results when an AI Overview is present (cited secondhand). calculator.net still gets about 52% of its traffic from Google organic (Semrush, Aug 2026), but it is a decades-old site. — [upGrowth AIO breakdown](https://upgrowth.in/ai-overviews-traffic-loss-2026-vertical-breakdown/); [Semrush: calculator.net](https://www.semrush.com/website/calculator.net/overview/)

### Inferences
- The **median verified small AI product sits around $177/month**, so reaching $200/month is roughly "median-of-survivors" performance among founders who list on TrustMRR, not the median of everyone who launches.
- In every documented fully-autonomous attempt I found, roughly 6 or more of them from 2026, sales landed between $0 and $54. Volume posting did not fix that (280 posts led to $0). Whatever sales did happen came from a **searchable, high-intent technical artifact**: a dev.to article plus a GitHub repo pointing at a cheap Gumroad product. That points to SEO-durable, developer-facing content as the one non-spam channel an agent can run alone, and it is slow.
- Mass programmatic SEO is now a high-risk channel for an agent. Several 2026 Google updates specifically hit template/AI-at-scale sites. A small number of genuinely useful tool pages with unique data or functionality is still viable; thousands of generated pages are not.
- Timeline: if an agent product does reach $200/month, expect something like 4–12 months, given the TrustMRR age–revenue correlation, the 124-day path to $1K MRR even for an audience-rich founder, and SEO lag. A 30–60 day horizon is very unlikely to show it.

### Gaps
- I could not open TrustMRR's API or stats page to compute the exact share of listings above $200/month, or the median time from listing to $200. A direct pull from the TrustMRR API (`/docs/api/list-startups`) would answer this.
- I found no Indie Hackers or MicroConf primary survey stating "% of products that ever reach $X/month." The figures quoted are blog estimates.
- I found no verified (Stripe-dashboard) case of a *fully autonomous* agent product reaching $200/month. Every claim of agent success at that level was self-reported or unverified, e.g. a Substack claim of "85 apps in a weekend, $1,000/day" with no evidence: [Revenue Engineered](https://revenueengineered.substack.com/p/when-the-ai-agent-is-the-business).

## Q2. Which product types (B2B utility, niche data, templates, AI-built apps) convert best for unknown sellers?

### Takeaway
The data is skewed and mostly estimated, but two patterns hold. Digital templates are extremely power-law: the median Notion template earns about $520 *lifetime*, and 44% of Gumroad products earn $0. Products sold *inside* a platform marketplace (Shopify apps, Chrome Web Store, Framer, Notion Marketplace) get built-in discovery but are still power-law. The famous "vibe-coded" revenue wins (e.g., fly.pieter.com) were driven by founders with huge pre-existing audiences, so they are not replicable by an unknown seller.

### Cited Findings
**Famous AI-built / vibe-coded revenue cases**
- fly.pieter.com, Pieter Levels' browser flight game (built Feb–Mar 2025 with Cursor, Three.js and Grok-generated server code). Levels showed the numbers publicly on X and via his live revenue posts. That is not Stripe-API verified by a third party, so treat it as **SELF-REPORTED (public dashboard/screenshots)**. Distribution was almost entirely his roughly 600K+ X following and the virality of the build-in-public spectacle. One roundup says Levels now lists it as "made money but not sustainable." I saw that only in a search summary and could not pin it to a primary Levels post. A separate headline reports "$5,000 per month," which conflicts with the other figures and may be from a different date. — [Dev.to: Pieter Levels failures](https://dev.to/idonthaveapen/failures-behind-a-420kmonth-solo-founder-i-read-all-751-of-pieter-levels-blog-posts-1h3b); [AvGeekery](https://avgeekery.com/?p=39779); [Stealwhatworks: vibecoded apps >$10K](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/vibecoded-apps-over-10k); [Questera roundup](https://www.questera.ai/blogs/top-10-vibe-coding-projects-that-make-you-real-money)

**Micro-SaaS by type**
- TrustMRR category totals put E-commerce first ($454.3K) and Content Creation second ($234.9K). BigIdeasDB says Content Creation startups average about $13.8K MRR versus about $1.5K for AI startups. [VERIFIED base; ESTIMATE analysis] — [TrustMRR stats](https://trustmrr.com/stats); [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/trustmrr)
- Small niche B2B examples from a micro-SaaS newsletter: CopyCopter (AI video) took about 6 weeks to reach $500; InstaRent (Netherlands rental matching) got its first paying user in Dec 2024 and had 34 paying users by Jan 2025; Notionsocial launched via Notion Facebook groups. [SELF-REPORTED; the publisher sells courses] — [Micro SaaS Idea](https://microsaasidea.substack.com/sitemap/2025)

### Inferences
- For an unknown seller, a one-off template is a weak bet: the median lifetime revenue of about $520 is roughly 2.6 months of the $200/month target, even for listings that sell at all. Catalog breadth (11+ products) correlates strongly with sales, which suits an agent that can produce many *genuinely useful* variants. The correlation is confounded by seller reputation, though.
- Niche B2B utilities with a clear buyer and a recurring price (paid by businesses) are probably the best fit for a $200/month target: 4–10 customers at $20–50/month. Consumer apps and games depend on virality or audience, which an agent lacks.
- "Vibe-coded success stories" are almost all **audience-led**. The AI-built part reduced build cost; it did not create demand.

### Gaps
- I found no verified dataset comparing conversion rates of B2B utilities, data products and templates for sellers with no audience.
- I found no primary, current fly.pieter.com revenue trend for late 2025 to 2026.

## Q3. Marketplace-native distribution (Shopify, Chrome, Notion, Framer, Slack, Figma) vs products needing external marketing

### Takeaway
Marketplaces solve the "zero traffic" problem partly by bringing buyers who are already searching. They are still power-law, and most listings earn little. They are the most plausible non-spam distribution for an unknown, autonomous seller, but they require owner-level account setup, review and approval, and sometimes waitlists.

### Cited Findings
- **Shopify App Store:** Shopify takes 0% revenue share on a developer's first $1M lifetime revenue, then 15%. Earnings before 1 Jan 2025 don't count toward that threshold, and the limit is pooled across associated accounts. [VERIFIED official] — [Shopify dev changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/update-to-shopifys-app-developer-revenue-share). The store lists about 13–17.6K apps (April 2026), adding about 550 per month. 32% of apps have no paid plan. The median app reportedly earns under $1K/month, and "a meaningful chunk earn nothing." [ESTIMATE/VENDOR] — [GapQuery](https://www.gapquery.com/blog/how-much-do-shopify-apps-make); [WeekOne Labs benchmarks 2026](https://weekonelabs.com/blog/shopify-app-revenue-benchmarks-2026/); [App Store Research](https://appstoreresearch.com/blog/best-new-shopify-apps)
- **Chrome Web Store:** a Feb 2024 analysis found 69.53% of extensions have fewer than 100 users and only 7.66% have more than 1,000. Self-reported indie wins include $3,700/month, and $15K/month from 700K users. Hosting is free and organic store search drives discovery, but the store doesn't process payments (ExtensionPay and similar services fill that gap). [SELF-REPORTED; older data] — [ExtensionPay article](https://extensionpay.com/articles/browser-extensions-make-money); [Tinkrd](https://tinkrd.com/can-you-make-money-from-building-a-chrome-extension/); [Indie Hackers: $400 extension](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/chatbackup/my-chrome-extension-just-hit-400-in-revenue--O99mP9fYQ_nLax5tEDS)
- **Framer Marketplace:** creators keep 100% of paid template sales, and templates also earn referral commissions when buyers upgrade via a remix link. Framer reportedly paid creators about $6.5M in 2025. A blog estimates a first template earns $500–$2,000 *total*. Template-level sales are hard to verify publicly. [Official terms; ESTIMATE earnings] — [Framer creator program](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/how-the-creator-program-works/); [All About Framer](https://allaboutframer.com/how-much-can-you-actually-earn-with-framer-in-2026-(real-numbers)); [Stealwhatworks: Framer >$10K](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/framer-templates-over-10k-month-now)
- **Notion Marketplace:** the fee is 10% plus $0.40 per transaction (US page; some locales show 8%). Payouts are biweekly with a $20 minimum and a 14-day hold. Sellers must **join a waitlist, be approved by Notion, and onboard with Stripe**. I found no official earnings or discovery data; the earnings claims come from competing platforms (Fungies, Payhip). — [Notion help: selling on Marketplace](https://www.notion.com/help/selling-on-marketplace); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/sell-notion-templates/)
- **Gumroad Discover** (the internal marketplace) requires a first sale to unlock. One agent experiment bought its own product for £1 to unlock it. — (search summary of [DEV/HN experiments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47417016))
- Freemius/MicroConf: integrations, partnerships and communities were rated more dependable than paid ads by 47% of independent SaaS founders in 2025. — [Shno](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/micro-saas-launch-statistics)

### Inferences
- For an agent with no audience, a marketplace listing (Shopify app, Chrome extension, Framer/Notion template, possibly WordPress plugins or VS Code extensions) is the closest thing to "distribution without marketing." The owner must handle partner-account creation, KYC/tax forms and sometimes manual approval or waitlists, and listing quality and reviews compound slowly.
- Shopify apps match the $200/month target best: merchants already pay monthly, and 0% revenue share applies at this scale. They also carry the heaviest support and review burden (app review, GDPR webhooks, merchant support).

### Gaps
- I found no verified, platform-published distribution of seller earnings for Notion Marketplace, Framer, Figma Community, Slack or WordPress.
- I found no data on how long a new listing takes to get its first organic sale in each marketplace.

## Q4. What owner involvement (social posting, Product Hunt launch, replying to users) proved necessary? How much ongoing human effort?

### Takeaway
In every success case I found, a human with an audience or with relationships drove distribution (Levels and Lou on X, a PM's Instagram reel, founders posting in Facebook groups or Reddit). Fully autonomous runs with only light human help (logins, forms) produced $0–$54. Payment setup, marketplace approval, attribution and support are recurring points where humans had to step in.

### Cited Findings
- In one autonomous Claude agent experiment, a human "copilot" handled logins and forms. The agent built a $5 starter kit on Payhip, and at publication had no first sale yet. — [DEV: $0 agent business](https://dev.to/m_ashrey122/i-gave-an-ai-agent-0-and-told-it-to-build-a-business-heres-what-it-did-first-4k5c)
- The Automaton Agency teardown focuses on where a human had to intervene, and concludes that agents cut the cost of doing, not the cost of being right. — [Automaton Agency](https://automatonagency.com/insights/autonomous-agent-revenue-experiment-teardown)

### Inferences
- The minimum owner involvement that plausibly changes outcomes has three parts. (1) One-time account, KYC and marketplace approvals. (2) Possibly letting the product be posted under the owner's real identity in 2–4 relevant communities: a Show HN, one niche subreddit following its self-promotion rules, a relevant Discord. Agents posting as anonymous new accounts tend to get filtered or banned, and that drifts toward spam. (3) Answering a handful of early customer emails, or delegating that to the agent with oversight. Without (2), the evidence suggests only slow organic/marketplace search can work.
- Measuring attribution from day one (UTM tags, referrer logging) is a cheap, repeated lesson from the agent experiments.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled comparison of "agent-only distribution" vs "agent plus owner posting once" for the same product.
- I found no reliable data on the ongoing support hours for a $200/month micro-product. Anecdotes suggest it is low for templates and medium for SaaS or Shopify apps.
